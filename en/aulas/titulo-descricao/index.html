Lesson 4 · 16 min

Impression without a click: rewrite the title and description

A page with lots of impressions and few clicks seems to need a new title. Sometimes it does; sometimes the impressions come from people who searched for your brand and clicked the homepage, and the title is not to blame. In this lesson you look at where the impressions come from before changing anything, rewrite only what's worth it, and measure for two weeks.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1Filter by page and see the queries behind the impressions
  2. 2When the title isn't the problem
  3. 3Write a title and description with a specific promise
  4. 4Measure the change without fooling yourself

Goal: find out where a page's impressions come from before rewriting the title and description, and measure the change for two weeks.

02 · From symptom to measured change

Click each step.

1. The suspicion

List the pages with the most impressions and low CTR over the last 28 days.

Use the non-branded file from lesson 2: it already removes the brand queries.

At INEMA (baseline from 09/23/2026)The page /guias/trilha-iniciantes.html had 730 impressions and 4 clicks at position 1.7. The baseline report flagged it as a "quick title win".

2. The queries

Filter by page and read the queries. Add up how much is branded.

If it's almost all branded, the "weak title" diagnosis falls apart.

At INEMAWhen the page's queries were opened, almost all the impressions came from searches for the brand. The title proposal ended up light: say what the page is, for when topic searches show up. It stayed as a draft, with no result measurement.

3. The rewrite

Title: what the person gets, with a number or a concrete scope, in about 60 characters or fewer. Description: what's on the page, in one or two sentences.

Don't promise what the page doesn't deliver.

4. The measurement

Note the date of the change, the CTR and the position from before. Wait two weeks before judging or changing again.

If your position changed a lot during the period, the CTR comparison isn't valid.

Made-up example from a ceramics shop: the new title says what the person gets and the angle.

  • Changing the title of a page that only shows up for the brand: it changes nothing.
  • Changing it again after 3 days: there's no time to measure.
  • Clickbait title: the click goes back to Google and the page loses.

04 · Practice: one swap with a diagnosis

E2 asks for a two-week wait.

E1 · Three suspects diagnosed

30 min

Pick 3 pages with impressions and low CTR. For each one, open the queries and classify them: branded, non-branded far, non-branded close.

Done when: each of the 3 pages has its classification and its 3 queries with the most impressions noted down.

E2 · One measured swap

30 min

On the "non-branded, close" page (if there is one), rewrite the title and description and fill in the log. Two weeks later, complete the "after" column.

Done when: the log has the swap date, before and after numbers, and a written decision; or one sentence saying why none of the 3 needed a swap.
